## Queue

<Diagram name="mix-queue" rows={1} height={260} caption="A landing bounces a square once, and an idle loop that pulses its scale is queued after it with a mix of 0.2 seconds. Pick a delay to see where the idle loop starts. The light band is the mix." />

`queue` plays an animation after what the track holds now, with no gap between them. On an empty track it plays at once. A short animation that ends in a loop is the common case: a bounce and then rest, a hit and then idle.

At `delay 0` the mix ends exactly where the landing ends, so the bounce plays whole and the pulse takes over as it lands. At `delay -0.2` the idle loop starts while the square is still in the air, and the bounce is cut short. At `delay 0.15` it starts 0.15 seconds after the landing started, which is how a reaction can begin before the action is over.

<Snippet source={source} regions="queue" />

`delay` above 0 starts the queued animation that many seconds after the one before it started. At 0 or below it counts from the end of the one before, less the mix, so a negative delay starts it a little early.

## Mix

When one animation replaces another, the pose would jump from one to the other. A mix blends them over some time instead: the outgoing animation fades while the incoming one grows.

<Diagram name="mix-time" rows={1} height={260} caption="One track plays A, which moves a square right, then B, which scales it, then A again. Pick a mix to see how long the change takes. The light bands are the mixes." />

Pick **0 s** first. The square jumps: one frame it is to the right, the next it is home and scaling. With **0.5 s** it slides home while it starts to scale. Each value goes from where the old animation has it to where the new one has it, a little further every frame.

A value that the new animation does not key goes back to the setup pose over the mix. B keys no position, so the square slides home. If B moved the square too, it would slide from where A had it to where B has it.

<Snippet source={source} regions="mix" />

The mix comes from three places, and the most specific wins:

- `mix` of `play` or `queue` holds for that one call.
- `setMix` holds for one pair of animations.
- `defaultMix` holds for every other pair. It is 0 unless you set it.

`mix: 0` cuts at once. Use it where the change has to happen on a beat, such as a bounce that must start the moment something lands.

A mix blends what moves: bones, colors and deforms. The image a slot shows does not fade. By default it switches when the mix starts, and the entry field `mixAttachmentThreshold` moves that point.

## Layers

<Diagram name="mix-layers" height={170} rows={1} caption="Track 0 moves a square right, track 1 scales it. Turn track 1 off and on." />

Each track holds one animation, and a higher track applies over the lower ones. An animation on track 1 sets the bones and slots it keys, and the others keep what track 0 gives them. So an overlay can wave a hand while the legs keep walking.

The two tracks key different values, so nothing competes: the square moves and scales at once. Turn track 1 off, and the scale goes back to what track 0 gives it, which is the setup pose, because track 0 keys no scale.

<Snippet source={source} regions="layers" />

When two tracks key the same value, the higher one decides how they combine:

<Diagram name="mix-same" height={170} rows={1} caption="Track 0 scales a square up, track 1 scales it down. Pick how track 1 applies." />

- **replace**, the default: track 1 wins. The square only shrinks, as if track 0 were not there.
- **alpha 0.5**: track 1 applies half way. The scale sits between what track 0 and track 1 give, so the two partly cancel.
- **additive**: track 1 adds its change from the setup pose. Track 0 grows the square by 0.8 and track 1 shrinks it by 0.5, so the result grows by 0.3 at most.

`alpha` sets the share in code, and `additive: true` makes a track add. An additive track suits a change that should come on top of anything, such as a breath or a recoil.

<Snippet source={source} regions="fade" />

`stop` with a mix fades a track out. A higher track fades back to what the tracks below it show.

## What a mix costs

During a mix both animations apply in every update, so a mix costs about two animations for its length. A track costs what its animation keys. [An animation](/how/animation/) shows how a time becomes a pose.
