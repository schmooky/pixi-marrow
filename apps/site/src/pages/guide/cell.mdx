## Skeleton units are not pixels

The animator draws in the units of the Spine editor. A symbol 300 units wide shows 300 pixels wide at scale 1. The cells of the reels have the size of the game layout, so every symbol needs a scale to fit.

The origin of a skeleton is where the animator put the root bone. For a symbol that is often its middle, but not always. So place a symbol by its box, not by its origin.

## Measure the setup pose once

<Snippet source={source} regions="measure" />

Measure the setup pose, not the current frame. A win glows and a landing bounces, so the box of a playing symbol changes every frame, and a symbol fitted to it would jump. Measure once per symbol and keep the box with the asset.

## Fit the cell

<Snippet source={source} regions="fit" />

The box of an `A` is smaller than the box of a coin. Fitted each by its own box, an `A` grows and a coin shrinks, and the set no longer looks as the designer drew it.

## Or use the design frame

<Snippet source={source} regions="design" />

Most symbol sets are drawn on one frame, such as 256 by 256 units, with the glow of a win reaching beyond it. Ask the animator for that frame. One scale for the whole set keeps the sizes the designer chose, and a glow may overflow its cell as intended.

## What the scale changes

The scale of the container scales the picture. The skeleton inside it keeps its units, so everything you read from it is in skeleton units, in the space of the symbol:

- `bone(name).worldX` and `worldY`, and the points of `localToWorld`.
- `getLocalBounds()`.
- `physicsTranslate`, which takes the distance the symbol moved. A reel that moves a symbol 20 pixels moves it `20 / scale` units.

<Snippet source={source} regions="units" />

`toGlobal` turns a point of the symbol into a point of the stage, with the scale and the place of the symbol. A container that `follow` or `attach` puts into the symbol is scaled with it, so a value on a coin shrinks with the coin.

The scale is also the scale of the container that a game tweens. A win that pops a symbol to 1.2 times its size multiplies the base scale of the symbol, and goes back to it.
