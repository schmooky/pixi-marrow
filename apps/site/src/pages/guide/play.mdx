## An animation is a stretch of time

An animation has a length, such as 1.33 seconds. Playing it puts it on a track, and the track has a playhead that moves through that length as time passes. In every frame the runtime reads the pose at the playhead and draws it, as [One frame](/how/frame/) shows. Everything on this page changes how the playhead moves.

## Know the names

An animator gives each animation a name, and a game plays them by name. Names differ between studios, so read them from the files.

<Snippet source={source} regions="names" />

`info` lists the animations, skins, bones, slots and events of a skeleton, with the length of each animation. For names that TypeScript checks, `pixi-marrow typegen` writes them as types at build time.

A skeleton usually has one animation per state it can be in. The symbols of Bzzz are an example:

| Animation | What it shows | Length |
|---|---|---|
| `idle` | Rest. It loops. | 1.33 s |
| `blur_in` | Turns blurred, and stays so. | 0.27 s |
| `blur_out` | Turns sharp again. | 0.27 s |
| `landing` | A short bounce. | 0.33 s |
| `win` | A celebration. | 1.33 s |
| `disable` | A dimmed pose. | 0 s |

## Loop or hold

<Diagram name="play-loop" height={250} rows={0} caption="One animation that moves a square right in one second, played three ways. Watch what each does when the second is over." />

The first second, all of them move. Then they part:

- With `loop: true` the playhead jumps back to the start, and the move plays again.
- With `loop: false` the playhead stops at the end, and the square **holds its last frame**. It does not go back to the setup pose.
- An animation of 0 seconds has only a last frame, so it is a pose that holds from the start.

<Snippet source={source} regions="loop hold" />

`play` starts an animation now, on track 0 unless you name another, in place of what the track played. Animators use 0-second animations for states that do not move, such as `disable`, which dims a Bzzz symbol and keeps it dimmed.

## Speed and part

<Diagram name="play-speed" height={250} rows={0} caption="The same one-second move at speed 1, at speed 0.5, and with only its middle half played." />

`speed` makes the playhead move faster or slower: at 0.5 the move takes two seconds. `start` and `end` cut a part out of the animation, and a loop repeats that part only. `reverse` moves the playhead backwards. `timeScale` sets the speed of the whole skeleton, every track at once.

## Stop

<Snippet source={source} regions="stop" />

`stop` takes the animation off a track, or off all tracks when you name none. Without a mix the pose stays as the track left it, because nothing changes it any more. With a mix the track fades back to the setup pose over that time.

## Know when it ends

<Diagram name="play-finished" height={250} rows={0} caption="A win of 1.2 seconds, played whole on the left and replaced by another animation at 0.6 seconds on the right." />

`play` returns a `Track`, the handle on that animation. Its `finished` promise resolves when the animation has played to its end. It also resolves when something else takes its place first, as on the right. Code that waits for a win before it shows the total must therefore check whether the win was cut short. [Events](/guide/events/) shows how.
