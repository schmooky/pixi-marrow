## Keys the animator placed

An animator marks moments in an animation with event keys: a footstep, a hit, the moment a coin is collected. An event key changes nothing in the picture. It is a mark on the time of the animation, and the runtime tells the game when the playhead passes it.

<Diagram name="events-keys" height={240} rows={1} caption="An animation with two event keys, played twice. Each key writes a line when the playhead passes it. In the second pass the animation mixes out from 0.7 seconds." />

In the first pass both keys fire. In the second, another animation takes over at 0.7 seconds, and `hit` at 0.9 does not fire: an animation that mixes out fires no event, unless its `eventThreshold` allows it. The game listens for the keys instead of guessing times in code, so when the animator moves the hit, the sound moves with it.

<Snippet source={source} regions="keys" />

The coin of Bzzz fires `onCollect` 0.23 seconds into its `collect`, and `onSound` 0.53 seconds into its `second_chance`. The bee fires `onBeeWow` 0.33 seconds into its `win`.

An event carries a name and the values the animator set: `int`, `float`, `string`, and `volume` and `balance` for an event with audio. It also carries `time`, where it sits in the animation. The listener gets the `Track` of the animation as well.

An event fires in the update in which the playhead passes it. At 60 frames a second the playhead moves about 0.017 seconds a frame, so an event fires within one frame of its time.

## The life of a track

Besides the keys of the animator, the runtime reports what happens to each animation on a track.

<Diagram name="events-life" height={200} rows={1} caption="An idle loop of one second, replaced at 2.4 seconds by a win with a mix of 0.4 seconds. Each report appears when it happens." />

Each animation goes through these, in order:

- `start` when it begins to play.
- `complete` each time it reaches its end. A loop completes once per loop.
- `interrupt` when another animation replaces it, before its mix out.
- `end` when it leaves the track, after its mix out.
- `dispose` when the runtime drops it, after `end`.

The idle above reports `complete` at 1 and 2 seconds, once per loop. At 2.4 seconds it reports `interrupt` and the win reports `start`. The idle still shows during the mix, so its `end` comes at 2.8 seconds, when the mix is over.

## Waiting for an animation

<Snippet source={source} regions="cut" />

`finished` resolves when an animation that does not loop has played to its end, or when it leaves the track for any other reason. To know which it was, listen for `interrupt` and compare its `Track` with the one `play` returned. That keeps a sequence from going on after something else took over the skeleton.

## Cost

An event key costs nothing while nobody listens for `event`. The runtime collects the events of an update and emits them after it, so a listener can call `play` safely.
