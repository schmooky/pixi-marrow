## Skeleton units are not pixels

The animator draws in the units of the Spine editor. A skeleton 300 units wide shows 300 pixels wide at scale 1. The layout of a game has its own sizes, so most skeletons need a scale.

<Diagram name="size-units" height={210} rows={3} caption="A skeleton with a bone 100 units right of its origin. Pick a scale for its container." />

The picture grows and shrinks with the scale. The bone does not move inside the skeleton: it stays 100 units from the origin. Only its place on the stage changes. `toGlobal` computes that place with the scale.

The origin of a skeleton is where the animator put the root bone. That can be its middle, its feet or a corner. So place a skeleton by its box, not by its origin.

## Measure the setup pose once

<Diagram name="size-box" height={200} rows={0} caption="A square that grows and lifts, fitted into a cell two ways." />

On the left, the game fits the square by its box in each frame. That box hugs the square. So the fit shrinks the square as much as it grows, and centres it again when it lifts. The animation disappears. On the right, the game fits the square once, by the box of its setup pose. The animation plays as the animator made it.

With a real skeleton, whose parts move differently, a fit per frame also makes it jitter. Measure the setup pose once per asset, and keep the box with it.

## Fit a box

<Snippet source={source} regions="fit" />

Fitted each by its own box, a small skeleton grows and a large one shrinks. Within a set of skeletons that belong together, that changes the sizes the designer chose.

## Or use the design frame

<Snippet source={source} regions="design" />

Animators often draw skeletons that belong together on one frame, such as the symbols of a slot game on 256 by 256 units. Ask the animator for that frame. One scale for the whole set keeps the sizes that the designer chose. A glow may reach beyond the frame, as intended.

## What the scale changes

The scale of the container scales the picture. The skeleton inside keeps its units, so what you read from it is in skeleton units, in the space of the display object:

- `bone(name).worldX` and `worldY`, and the points of `localToWorld`.
- `getLocalBounds()`.
- `physicsTranslate`, which takes the distance the skeleton moved. Moved 20 pixels on the stage, it moved `20 / scale` units.

<Snippet source={source} regions="units" />

`toGlobal` turns a point of the skeleton into a point of the stage, with its scale and place. A container that `follow` or `attach` puts into the skeleton scales with it.

The scale is also the one a game tweens. A pop to 1.2 times the size multiplies the base scale, and returns to it.

The official runtime can also scale a skeleton while it loads, so that bones and bounds are in pixels. In pixi-marrow, a skeleton scales with its container only.
