Most of the speed of pixi-marrow needs no option. See [what is automatic](/good-to-know/#what-is-automatic). The settings on this page change the timing, the memory or the picture, so each one waits until you ask for it.

## Presets

<Snippet source={source} regions="preset" />

`perf` takes `exact`, `balanced` or `fast`. `exact` is the default and changes nothing. A skeleton then looks and moves as in the official runtime.

| Setting | `exact` | `balanced` | `fast` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tightMesh` | `auto` | `on` | `on` |
| `bake` | `off` | `auto` | `auto` |
| `idleCache` | `off` | `auto` | `auto` |
| `offscreen` | `update` | `throttle` | `pause` |
| `updateRate` | `display` | `display` | 30 for batched skeletons |
| `lod` | `off` | `off` | `auto` |
| `skinning` | `auto` | `auto` | `auto` |

## Change one setting

<Snippet source={source} regions="settings" />

A `perf` object starts from its `preset` and changes the settings it names. An option given by name wins over both. A value that does not exist throws a `MarrowError` with the closest valid name.

## The settings

- **`offscreen`.** A skeleton is off the screen when its bounds lie outside the screen, or when no render reached it for a tenth of a second. `throttle` updates it 4 times a second with the time that passed, so events fire late. `pause` stops its time until it is back.
- **`updateRate`.** A number poses the skeleton at most that many times a second, with the time of the frames in between. Motion steps at low rates. The rate of `fast` applies to batched skeletons only, unless you set it by name.
- **`lod`.** `auto` leaves physics and deform out while the skeleton is drawn smaller than 96 pixels across. Meshes then show their setup shape. Both come back at 120 pixels, physics with a reset, so nothing jumps.
- **`idleCache`.** `auto` draws a skeleton that rested for 30 updates as one image, until its pose or its place changes. The image takes 4 bytes per pixel of its box on the screen. A slot that multiplies or screens keeps the skeleton live.
- **`tightMesh`.** `on` draws every region as the polygon around its visible pixels. Some pixels move by 1 of 255 levels.
- **`bake`.** `auto` plays an animation from frames baked once, while it plays alone on track 0. The engine still runs the tracks and fires the events. Between two frames, 60 a second, the frame before is shown. A skin with physics or a slider is never baked.
- **`skinning`.** `gpu` places the vertices of the mesh mode in the vertex shader from the bones, so a moving skeleton uploads its bones and not its vertices. Copies of one rig that follow each other in the display list then draw in one instanced call, which `instancing: 'off'` turns off. That pays for many copies of one rig. A grid of different symbols draws in fewer calls in the batched mode, so `auto` stays `cpu`.

## Bake at build time

<Snippet source={source} regions="bake" />

`bakeAnimations` of `pixi-marrow/bake` runs in Node too, so the clips can be baked at build time and shipped as a file. `addBakedClips` hands them to an asset, and its instances play them in place of baking. A clip costs 23 KB to 0.9 MB on the example rigs.

## See which are in effect

<Snippet source={source} regions="report" />

The first two rows of the report are `perf` and `pacing`. `perf` names the preset, the settings, and the ones you gave by name. `pacing` says how often the skeleton updates right now, and why. `pacingStatus()` returns the same as an object.

## Not measured yet

The presets were measured on one desktop machine. There are no numbers for phones yet, so `skinning: 'auto'` stays on the CPU and no preset changes it.

The [lab](/lab/) measures them on the device that opens it. Each test draws a scene with every side in turn and shows the times at the end, with a text to copy. Open a test on a phone and wait about a minute.
