## What is automatic

- **Skeletons share draw calls.** Skeletons of up to 1024 vertices are batched: 200 instances of a small rig draw in one call. A Pixi object between two skeletons in the draw order ends a batch, so keep skeletons next to each other in the display list.
- **A skeleton at rest costs close to nothing.** When its animation ended, or it never played one, no timeline runs and nothing is uploaded. It wakes with the next `play`.
- **Less overdraw.** After a load the page images are read in idle time. From then on a region without a visible pixel is not drawn, and a slot behind the opaque part of a later slot is skipped. The picture stays the same.

## Render mode

<Snippet source={source} regions="render-mode" />

`renderMode` is `auto` by default: a skeleton of up to 1024 vertices is batched, and a larger one gets meshes of its own. `mesh` and `batched` force one mode. `stats.renderMode` names the mode in use.

## Physics

<Snippet source={source} regions="physics" />

`physics` is the official mode: `update`, `pose`, `reset` or `none`. Physics does not follow the Pixi position by itself, so tell it with `physicsTranslate` and `physicsRotate`. A skeleton with physics constraints keeps simulating at rest.

## Versions

| What | Supported |
|---|---|
| Spine | Exports of 4.1, 4.2 and 4.3, as `.json` or `.skel` |
| PixiJS | Version 8, from 8.16 |
| Renderer | WebGL2 and WebGPU |

Every constraint is solved: IK, transform, path, physics and sliders. Clipping works.

## Limits

- No Canvas 2D and no WebGL1.
- No Spine 3.8 and no Spine 4.0.
- The runtime plays skeletons. Editing them stays in the Spine editor.
- The speed was measured on one desktop machine. There are no numbers for phones yet.
- The licence is the Spine Runtimes License, as for the official runtimes. Each user of a product built on it needs a Spine Editor licence of their own.
