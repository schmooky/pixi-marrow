## Draw order

Inside a skeleton, the slots draw from the back to the front, as the anatomy page showed. Between skeletons, Pixi decides. The children of a container draw in order, and the last child draws on top.

Every two seconds a symbol wins in the preview. It grows a little, so it must draw over its neighbours. The preview moves it to the end of the container with `reels.addChild(symbol)`, and the counter stays at one.

The draw call count stays, but the move is not free. A change of the order makes Pixi build the instructions of that render group again. Each skeleton in it then writes itself into the batch again. Once every two seconds is fine. A game that changes the order in every frame pays that in every frame. `stats.instructionBuilds` counts the builds.

## Draw calls

The GPU draws when the CPU asks it to, with a draw call. A draw call names a shader, the textures to sample and how to blend the result over what is there. Each call costs time on the CPU and in the graphics driver, whatever it draws. A phone has less of that time than a desktop.

So the fewer calls a frame takes, the more of the frame remains for the game. The goal is to draw many skeletons in one call.

## A batch

<Diagram name="batches-calls" height={130} rows={1} caption="Six skeletons in the display list. The renderer walks them from first to last and makes draw calls. Add a text between them, or make one multiply." />

The renderer walks the display list in order and adds objects to the current batch while they fit. Each color is one draw call. With nothing added, all six skeletons fit into one. A text needs another shader, so it ends the batch and takes a call of its own. The skeletons after it start a third. A skeleton that multiplies needs another blend, and does the same.

The batched mode writes the triangles of skeletons that follow each other into one shared buffer, and draws them with one call. That works while all of them use:

- the same shader
- the same blend state
- at most {facts.batch.pages} atlas pages.

The Bzzz symbols share one page, so the 15 symbols take one call.

## Additive slots stay in the batch

<Diagram name="batches-blend" height={190} rows={1} caption="A pixel on the screen, a color painted over it at half cover, and the result. Pick how the slot blends." />

The glow of the coin is additive: it brightens what lies under it. Additive blending usually needs a blend state of its own. That would end the batch at every coin.

pixi-marrow draws with premultiplied alpha, where a color is already multiplied by its alpha. The GPU then blends with one rule: the new color, plus what lies under it times one minus the new alpha. A normal pixel covers what is under it by its alpha. An additive pixel writes an alpha of 0. So what lies under it stays whole, and the color adds to it. One rule serves both, and the coin stays in the batch.

One result follows from the alpha of 0. An additive slot adds no alpha to the target. Over an opaque background that changes nothing. In a transparent canvas or a render texture, a glow over nothing stays transparent in the alpha channel. What the page shows behind such a canvas then shines through the glow.

## What ends a batch

Use the switches one at a time:

- **text between** puts a Pixi text into the container, between two symbols. The frame takes 3 calls: the symbols before the text, the text, and the symbols after it. Keep texts, frames and effects above or below the symbols, never between them.
- **multiply** makes one symbol multiply what is under it. That needs another blend state, so the frame takes 3 calls again. A slot that multiplies or screens inside a skeleton ends the batch the same way. Additive slots do not.
- **meshes** changes every symbol to the mesh mode, where a skeleton draws its own mesh. The frame takes 15 calls. `renderMode: 'auto'` picks that mode for a skeleton of more than {facts.batch.autoVertices} vertices. It picks the batched mode for every smaller one.

Four more things end a batch, and the preview has no switch for them:

- **Too many pages.** A batch ends when its skeletons need more than {facts.batch.pages} atlas pages. So a set of skeletons on a few pages keeps its batches long. In the mesh mode one draw samples up to {facts.mesh.pages} pages.
- **A dark tint.** A skeleton with a dark tint on a slot draws with a second batcher, whose vertices carry one more color. So a batch ends where a skeleton with a dark tint follows one without. The other way round costs nothing: a skeleton without a dark tint joins the open batch of one with it. Keep skeletons with a dark tint next to each other in the display list.
- **A render group, a mask or a filter.** Each starts draw calls of its own in Pixi.
- **A container attached to a slot.** The skeleton ends its draw call at that slot.

A device can also refuse the batch. Some devices sample fewer textures in one draw than the pages one skeleton needs, up to {facts.batch.pages}. The skeleton then draws in the mesh mode, also with `renderMode: 'batched'`. `stats.renderMode` names the mode in use, and the `renderMode` row of `perf.report()` gives the reason.

## Uploads

Before a draw, the GPU needs the vertices. The batched mode keeps them in two buffers. Positions change with the pose. Texture coordinates, colors and flags change now and then.

Every skeleton notes the stretches of the buffer that it wrote. Before the draw, the runtime uploads them. How exact that is depends on the renderer and on the version of pixi.js:

| Mode | Renderer and pixi.js | What uploads |
|---|---|---|
| Batched | WebGPU, and WebGL2 from 8.23 | Each stretch that changed |
| Batched | WebGL2 on 8.21 and 8.22 | One stretch per buffer, from the first change to the last |
| Mesh | From 8.23 | The range that changed |
| Mesh | 8.21 and 8.22 | The whole buffer of the skeleton |

When many stretches changed, the runtime uploads the used part of the buffer whole, because that is cheaper than many small calls. After Pixi builds the instructions of a render group again, the buffers of its batches upload whole too.

A skeleton at rest uploads nothing, so a reel that stands still costs one draw call and no upload. That holds while no display object above the skeleton moves, fades or changes its tint. In the batched mode such a parent makes each skeleton below it write every vertex again. `stats.frameBytes` is 0 after a frame in which a skeleton sent nothing. [Performance caveats](/performance/caveats/#a-parent-that-moves-or-fades) has the fix, and the rule is [MARROW001](/performance/rules/#marrow001).

## What pixi-marrow does

pixi-marrow has two batchers of its own, one for skeletons with a dark tint and one for the others. An element of a batch is a whole skeleton, with up to {facts.batch.pages} pages of its atlas. The batch goes to the batch pipe of Pixi like any other batch. So skeletons mix with Pixi objects in the display list and keep their order.

`stats.drawCalls` gives the draw calls of one skeleton, and `perf.report()` says why it takes them.
