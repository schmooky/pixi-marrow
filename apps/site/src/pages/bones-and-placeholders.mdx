## Follow or attach

A game often needs its own objects on a skeleton: a number over a coin, a glow on a sword, a hat a player picked. There are two ways, and they differ in where the object is drawn.

<Diagram name="follow-attach" height={210} rows={1} caption="A skeleton of three slots and a star that goes with its bone. Pick how the star is added." />

With **follow**, the star moves with a bone but stays where it was in Pixi's display list, after the skeleton, so it draws over every slot. With **attach**, the star becomes part of the skeleton's draw order, after the slot it is attached to, so slots after that one cover it. Pick attach to see the front slot pass over the star.

## Attach to a slot

<Snippet source={source} regions="ring attach" />

`attach` makes the container a part of the skeleton. It is drawn at the place of the slot in the draw order, after the attachment of that slot. It moves with the bone of the slot, and its alpha is that of the slot.

A slot holds one container, and it shows on an empty slot too. That is what a placeholder slot of a rig is for. `detach` lets the container go.

<Snippet source={more} regions="options" />

`followAttachment` hides the container while the slot shows no attachment. `followColor` tints it with the color of the slot.

Each attached slot costs one more draw call, plus the draw calls of the container. A clipping attachment does not clip the container.

## Follow a bone

<Snippet source={source} regions="follow" />

`follow` gives a container the position, rotation, scale and skew of a bone after every update. The transform is in the space of the skeleton, so add the container to it first.

The container stays where it is in the display list, and following costs no draw call. `unfollow` ends it.

## Read and write a bone

<Snippet source={source} regions="bone" />

`bone` returns the handle of a bone, the same object at every call. `worldX`, `worldY` and `worldRotation` are read only. They say where the bone is in the skeleton after the last update, with y pointing down as in Pixi.

<Snippet source={more} regions="write" />

`x`, `y`, `rotation`, `scaleX` and `scaleY` are the pose next to the parent bone, as the Spine editor shows it. Rotation is in degrees.

A write takes effect with the next update. It stays until an animation that keys the bone writes it again.
