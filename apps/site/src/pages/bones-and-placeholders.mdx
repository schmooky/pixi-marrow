## Follow or attach

A game often needs its own objects on a skeleton: a number over a coin, a glow on a sword, a hat a player picked. There are two ways, and they differ in where Pixi draws the object.

<Diagram name="follow-attach" height={210} rows={1} caption="A skeleton of three slots and a star that goes with its bone. Pick how the game adds the star." />

With **follow**, the star moves with a bone but stays where it was in Pixi's display list, after the skeleton. So it draws over every slot. With **attach**, the star becomes part of the draw order of the skeleton, after the slot that holds it. So the slots after that one cover it. Pick attach to see the front slot pass over the star.

## Attach to a slot

<Snippet source={more} regions="import" />

<Snippet source={source} regions="ring attach" />

`attach` makes the container a part of the skeleton. It draws at the place of the slot in the draw order, after the attachment of that slot. It moves with the bone of the slot, and its alpha is that of the slot.

A slot holds one container, and it shows on an empty slot too. That is what a placeholder slot of a rig is for. `detach` removes the container from the slot.

<Snippet source={more} regions="options" />

`followAttachment` hides the container while the slot shows no attachment. `followColor` tints it with the color of the slot.

Each attached slot costs one more draw call, plus the draw calls of the container. The skeleton ends its draw call at the slot, Pixi draws the container, and the skeleton starts a new one. A clipping attachment does not clip the container. An attached container also stops instancing for its skeleton, and no slot counts as covered across it. See the rule [MARROW012](/performance/rules/#marrow012).

## Follow a bone

<Snippet source={source} regions="follow" />

`follow` gives a container the position, rotation, scale and skew of a bone after every update. The transform is in the space of the skeleton. So add the container to the skeleton first.

The container stays where it is in the display list, and `follow` costs no draw call. `unfollow` ends it. For a container that is not a child of the skeleton, `follow` warns once in the console.

## Read and write a bone

<Snippet source={source} regions="bone" />

`bone` returns the handle of a bone, the same object at every call. `worldX`, `worldY` and `worldRotation` are read-only. They say where the bone is in the skeleton after the last update, with y pointing down as in Pixi.

<Snippet source={more} regions="write" />

`x`, `y`, `rotation`, `scaleX` and `scaleY` are the pose relative to the parent bone, as the Spine editor shows it. Rotation is in degrees.

A write takes effect with the next update. It stays until an animation that keys the bone writes it again.
