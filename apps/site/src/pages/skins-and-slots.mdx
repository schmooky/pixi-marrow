## What a skin is

A slot shows one attachment at a time, by name. A skin is a set of attachments for slots. For example, a skin `knight` may hold a helmet for the slot `hat` and a sword for the slot `hand`. When the skin changes, the pictures change, and the bones and the animations stay. So one skeleton can be many characters or many versions of a symbol.

## Several skins at once

<Diagram name="skins-sets" height={200} rows={1} caption="Two skins over three slots. Pick which skins are active, and in which order." />

With one skin, a slot that it fills shows its attachment. A slot that it does not fill stays empty. With two, each slot takes the attachment of the later skin that has one. With `cap, knight`, the hat is the helmet, because knight comes later. The shirt is still blue, because only cap has a shirt.

<Snippet source={source} regions="skins" />

A list of skins is active as one skin. Each skin adds its attachments, bones and constraints, in the order of the list. Where two skins store an attachment under one slot and name, the later one wins.

That is what the official runtime does with a skin that `Skin.addSkin` fills. So a rig from the mix-and-match example works as it does there. All instances of an asset share the combined skin of a list. The runtime keeps a combined skin that no instance shows any more, so that a return to it builds nothing again. It frees that skin once 8 newer combinations are unused too. A write to `skins` puts the slots into their setup pose, as a write to `skin` does.

## Change what a slot shows

<Snippet source={source} regions="attachment" />

`slot` returns the handle of a slot, the same object at every call. `attachment` takes the name under which the skins store an attachment for that slot. The active skins come first, then the default skin. Null shows nothing. For a name that the skins do not have, `attachment` throws an error that lists the names they have.

## Tint a slot

<Snippet source={source} regions="color" />

`tint` is the color of the slot as a Pixi tint. `alpha` goes from 0 to 1. A slot with tint black in the editor also has `darkTint`.

A write to a slot stays until an animation that keys the slot writes it again. A setup pose of the slots ends it too.

## The setup pose

<Snippet source={source} regions="setup" />

`setupPose` puts the bones and the slots into their setup pose. `'bones'` or `'slots'` resets one half. The tracks keep playing. To hold the pose, stop them first.

## Points on a bone

<Snippet source={source} regions="points" />

`localToWorld` takes a point next to a bone into the space of the skeleton. `worldToLocal` goes the other way. Pixi's `toGlobal` and `toLocal` of the skeleton then convert it to the stage and back.
