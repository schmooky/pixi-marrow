For PixiJS v8 there are two Spine runtimes: the official `@esotericsoftware/spine-pixi-v8`, and pixi-marrow. Take the official runtime when you need a released runtime, the Canvas renderer or the full `spine-core` API. Take pixi-marrow when many skeletons, or skeletons at rest, cost your game too much frame time. pixi-spine is for PixiJS v7 and earlier.

pixi-marrow is before version 1.0. Its version is `0.2.0`, and its API can still change. Read [what pixi-marrow does not have](#not-in-pixi-marrow) before you choose it.

## How this page was checked

Each cell about pixi-marrow comes from its code, its tests or a measurement. Each cell about the official runtime comes from the source of `@esotericsoftware/spine-pixi-v8` 4.2.120 and 4.3.13. Those two versions are development dependencies of this repo. Each cell about pixi-spine comes from the README of its repository, read on 15 November 2026.

A cell says `Not checked` where none of these sources gives the answer. The measured numbers are from the run of 15 November 2026 on [Benchmarks](/benchmarks/): one Apple M4, Chrome 157, PixiJS 8.23.0.

## The table

A skeleton is the bones, slots, skins and animations of one Spine export. A draw call is one command from the CPU to the GPU to draw triangles. An instance is one display object of a skeleton.

## Notes on the rows

**Spine versions.** Esoteric Software states the rule for its runtimes in the [Spine versioning](https://esotericsoftware.com/spine-versioning) page. The major and minor version of the editor that exports a file must match the runtime. In pixi-marrow, the loader reads the version from each file and loads the reader and the pose engine for it. So a game can hold 4.1, 4.2 and 4.3 exports at once.

**Draw calls.** The numbers are for 200 instances that play, on WebGL2 and on WebGPU. coin-pro has a slot that adds, and tank-pro has slots that multiply. In pixi-marrow a slot that multiplies or screens ends a batch too. [Draw order and batches](/how/batches/) shows what ends a batch.

**Cost at rest.** A skeleton is at rest when its animation ended and nothing changes its pose. pixi-marrow then runs no timeline and places no vertex. The official runtime poses and packs its vertices in each frame. A moving parent changes this for pixi-marrow: see [the question on render groups](/faq/#how-do-i-move-or-fade-many-skeletons-without-a-cost).

**CPU time while playing.** pixi-marrow took 0.23 to 0.77 times the CPU time of the official runtime, and one of those ratios is not decided. Its `update` call alone is slower on four of five rigs, because it places its vertices there. [Benchmarks](/benchmarks/) has every row.

**Size of the code.** pixi-marrow loads more code than the official runtime of one Spine version. The import of `MarrowSpine` is 31 kB. The first skeleton then loads a reader of 8.6 to 9.1 kB and a pose engine of 32 or 34 kB. The mesh mode adds 18 kB and clipping adds 7.4 kB, each when a skeleton needs it. These sizes are in `scripts/facts.json`, and `pnpm size` measures them.

The official numbers are from a bundle of `import { Spine } from '@esotericsoftware/spine-pixi-v8'`, made on 15 November 2026. The bundler was rolldown 1.3.2. The bundle is minified and leaves `pixi.js` out, as the size check of this repo does. That bundle holds `spine-core` and the Canvas renderer of `spine-canvas`.

**API.** The official runtime gives a game the objects of `spine-core`. pixi-marrow has a smaller API of its own and does not ship `spine-core`. Its pose core is a port of the official Spine Runtimes. The parity suite compares its poses with `spine-core` 4.1.55, 4.2.120 and 4.3.13, and they must be equal to the bit. [Migrate](/migrate/) maps the calls.

## Not in pixi-marrow

The official runtime has these, and pixi-marrow does not:

- A version 1.0. pixi-marrow is `0.2.0`, and a minor version before 1.0 can break an API.
- Support from the maker of Spine.
- The Canvas renderer.
- PixiJS 8.16 to 8.20.
- The `spine-core` objects: `spine.skeleton`, `spine.state`, a `Skin` that the game builds, and constraints that the game changes.
- A listener on one track entry, and `getCurrent(track)`.
- Bounds providers, such as `SetupPoseBoundsProvider` and `SkinsAndAnimationBoundsProvider`.
- `skeletonBounds`, for hit tests against bounding box attachments.
- The load options `scale` and `allowMissingRegions`.
- The hooks `beforeUpdateWorldTransforms` and `afterUpdateWorldTransforms`.
- Single-file builds, as an ES module and as an IIFE.

The numbers of pixi-marrow come from one desktop machine. It has no numbers for phones yet.

## In pixi-marrow only

The official runtime does not have these, as far as its source shows:

- Spine 4.1, 4.2 and 4.3 exports in one package.
- A skeleton at rest that runs no timeline and uploads nothing.
- Slots that add in the same batch as normal slots.
- Cuts of overdraw from the outlines of an atlas: `outlines`, `occlusion` and `tightMesh`.
- The options `skinning: 'gpu'`, `instancing`, `bake`, `idleCache`, `lod`, `offscreen` and `updateRate`.
- A report of what a skeleton costs: `hero.stats`, `perf.report()` and `perf.memory()`.
- Names of animations, skins, bones, slots and events as types.
- `Track.finished`, a promise that says why an animation ended.

Each option has a cost. [Performance](/performance/) lists what each one trades.

## Which one to take

1. Your game runs on PixiJS v7 or earlier: take pixi-spine. pixi-marrow and the official runtime for v8 do not run there.
2. Your game must run on the Canvas renderer: take the official runtime.
3. Your code uses `spine.skeleton` and `spine.state` in many places: stay with the official runtime, or read [Migrate](/migrate/) first.
4. Your game shows many skeletons at once, as the symbols of a slot game: measure pixi-marrow in your scene.
5. Your game ships soon and cannot take a breaking change: take the official runtime, and look at pixi-marrow again at 1.0.
